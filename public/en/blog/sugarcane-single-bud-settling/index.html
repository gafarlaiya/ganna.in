Single-bud setts direct in the field or nursery settlings: know this difference before you save seed

9 November 2026 · Gafar Laiya, Gujarat Bio Organics Pvt. Ltd.

Do not plant single-bud setts directly in the field just to save seed. In a Punjab trial, germination and yield were lower than with 2 to 3-bud setts. To save seed, look at raising single-bud settlings in a nursery and transplanting them, and count the extra nursery and transplanting work.

Where is the difference?

Many farmers hear "single bud" and treat two different methods as one.

  • Single-bud setts direct in the field: A small piece goes straight into the furrow. Germination depends on field moisture and conditions.
  • Nursery settlings: A seedling is raised in a nursery from a single bud or bud chip, then transplanted. A 2025 Indian Farming article by ICAR-IARI authors says single-bud settlings are transplanted at 25 to 35 days and bud-chip settlings at 6 to 8 weeks.

What the Punjab trial found

A three-year trial on spring planting was run at the Punjab Agricultural University station in Faridkot. The results appeared in the Journal of Sugarcane Research in 2013.

  • 3-bud setts: germination 31.6%, yield 64.7 t per hectare.
  • 2-bud setts: germination 32.2%, yield 64.9 t per hectare.
  • 1-bud setts direct: germination 25.1%, yield 48.1 t per hectare.
These are trial figuresThey are three-year averages from one location, not a promise. In your field results vary with soil, water, weather, variety and management. Punjab and South Gujarat have different weather.

In this trial there was no real yield difference between 2 and 3 buds. So if 3-bud setts are not available, 2-bud setts are an option. But planting a single bud direct cut yield sharply in this trial.

How much seed does the nursery method save?

The figures in the 2025 Indian Farming article are:

  • Normal 2 to 3-bud setts: 6 to 8 t per hectare, or about 1 to 1.3 t per vigha.
  • Single-bud settlings (spaced transplanting): the article gives about 1 to 2 t per hectare, or roughly 160 to 320 kg per vigha.
  • Bud chip: 8 to 10 quintals per hectare, or about 130 to 160 kg per vigha.

The same article gives transplant spacing of 90 x 90 cm or 120 x 60 cm. It says seed is about 20% of total cultivation cost, so the saving is a real point. But with it come nursery space, water, daily care and transplanting labour. Work out both sides before you decide.

Which method should you choose?

  • If good seed is not short: Healthy 2 to 3-bud setts are the simple route. For details read Sugarcane planting.
  • If seed of a new variety is short: Nursery settlings are useful where you must cover more field with little seed.
  • For gap filling: Keeping a few polybag settlings ready makes the job easier. See How to fill gaps.
  • If you cannot look after a nursery: If you cannot give daily water and attention, stay with direct 2 to 3-bud setts.

With any method, healthy seed comes first. Do not take buds or setts from a field with red rot or smut. If you get germination gaps, also read Why germination gaps happen.

Questions and answers

Can single-bud setts be planted direct?

They can, but in a Punjab trial germination and yield were lower than with 2 to 3-bud setts. If you want to save seed, look at the nursery settling method.

At what age should nursery settlings be transplanted?

According to the Indian Farming article, single-bud settlings are transplanted at 25 to 35 days and bud-chip settlings at 6 to 8 weeks.

Does saving seed raise yield?

There is no such guarantee. The saving is in seed. Yield depends on soil, water, variety and management.
